Climate Glossary

Adiabatic: A process in which no heat is exchanged between a system and its surroundings. In adiabatic processes, temperature changes occur solely due to expansion or compression work.

Albedo: The reflectivity of a surface, defined as the fraction of incident radiation that is reflected. Values range from 0 (complete absorption) to 1 (complete reflection).

Clausius-Clapeyron equation: An equation describing the relationship between saturation vapor pressure and temperature: $$ \frac{de_s}{dT} = \frac{L_v e_s}{R_v T^2} $$ where $e_s$ is saturation vapor pressure, $T$ is temperature, $L_v$ is latent heat of vaporization, and $R_v$ is the gas constant for water vapor.

Convection: The vertical transport of heat and other properties by fluid motion. Convective motion arises from buoyancy differences in the fluid.

Equator: The line of latitude at 0 degrees, dividing the Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The equatorial region receives the most direct solar radiation throughout the year.

Hemisphere: Half of the Earth, typically divided into Northern and Southern Hemispheres by the equator, or Eastern and Western Hemispheres by the prime meridian.

Hydrostatic balance: The state in which the vertical pressure gradient in a fluid is balanced by the gravitational force per unit volume: $$ \frac{dp}{dz} = -\rho g $$ where $p$ is pressure, $z$ is altitude, $\rho$ is density, and $g$ is gravitational acceleration. In the atmosphere, this occurs when vertical accelerations are negligible.

Humidity: The amount of water vapor present in the air.

Ideal gas law: An equation of state relating pressure, density, and temperature for an ideal gas. For dry air: $p = \rho R_d T$, where $p$ is pressure, $\rho$ is density, $R_d$ is the specific gas constant for dry air, and $T$ is temperature.

Insolation: Incoming solar radiation received at the Earth's surface or atmosphere.

Isothermal: Characterized by constant temperature. An isothermal atmosphere is one in which temperature does not vary with altitude.

Lapse rate: The rate at which atmospheric temperature decreases with increasing altitude, defined as $\Gamma = -dT/dz$, where $T$ is temperature and $z$ is altitude.

Latitude: The angular distance north or south of the equator, measured in degrees from 0 (equator) to 90 (poles). Lines of constant latitude run parallel to the equator.

Longitude: The angular distance east or west of the prime meridian, measured in degrees from 0 to 180. Lines of constant longitude are called meridians and run from pole to pole. The prime meridian is the reference meridian at 0 degrees longitude, passing through Greenwich, England.

Mid-latitudes: The regions between approximately 30 and 60 degrees latitude in both hemispheres, characterized by variable weather patterns and the interaction of tropical and polar air masses.

Mixing ratio: The mass of water vapor per unit mass of dry air.

Polar regions: The regions poleward of approximately 60 degrees latitude in both hemispheres, characterized by low solar radiation, cold temperatures, and shallow tropospheres.

Radiative forcing: The net energy input to a system from radiative processes, typically measured as the difference between absorbed and emitted radiation.

Relative humidity: The ratio of the actual vapor pressure to the saturation vapor pressure at a given temperature.

Saturation vapor pressure: The maximum partial pressure of water vapor that can exist in equilibrium with liquid water at a given temperature.

Specific heat capacity: The amount of energy required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of a substance by one degree. Denoted $c_p$ at constant pressure and $c_v$ at constant volume.

Specific humidity: The mass of water vapor per unit mass of moist air.

Stratosphere: The layer of the atmosphere above the troposphere, extending from the tropopause to approximately 50 km altitude, characterized by increasing temperature with height.

Subtropics: The regions between approximately 23.5 and 35 degrees latitude in both hemispheres, located between the tropics and mid-latitudes, often characterized by descending air and relatively dry conditions.

Tropics: The regions between the Tropic of Cancer (23.5N) and the Tropic of Capricorn (23.5S), characterized by high solar radiation, warm temperatures, and deep convective activity.

Troposphere: The lowest layer of the atmosphere, extending from the surface to approximately 8-18 km altitude depending on latitude, in which temperature generally decreases with height and most weather phenomena occur.

Tropopause: The boundary between the troposphere and stratosphere, marking the transition from decreasing to increasing temperature with altitude.

Zonal: Referring to the east-west direction, along lines of latitude (zones).
